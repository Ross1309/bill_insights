How these numbers are counted

  • Same point in the term: each Congress starts on January 3 of an odd year; "day N" is N days after that, so every Congress is compared at the same stage of its two years.
  • Laws: public and private laws from Congress.gov, dated by the "Became Public/Private Law" action.
  • Bills introduced: measures that can become law (H.R., S., H.J.Res., S.J.Res.). Simple and concurrent resolutions, which can't, are left out. Earlier Congresses are counted up to the same day by introduction date.
  • Got a roll-call vote: those measures that had at least one recorded House or Senate vote (any question, not only final passage). Many bills pass by voice vote or unanimous consent without one.
  • Roll-call votes: every recorded vote: the House list from Congress.gov (House Clerk) and senate.gov's vote lists.
  • Parties split: most Democrats and most Republicans voted opposite ways (Yea vs Nay); ties left out.

Mockup notes: decisions for you

  • Where it lives: a "This Congress" tab (shown), replacing the separate Charts page; Home's "by the numbers" links here.
  • "Passed a chamber" is missing from the funnel. Congress.gov has no count by stage; getting it means reading the action history of every bill that left committee, a one-time backfill of a few thousand requests, then nightly upkeep. Worth it?
  • Party split has no comparison yet. The earlier Congresses need their vote-by-vote party totals (about 3,900 House Clerk and senate.gov records, one time).
  • After January 3 this page becomes "The 120th Congress so far" with the 119th as the nearest comparison.
